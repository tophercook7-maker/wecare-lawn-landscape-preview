How to fix a patchy, thin, or dead lawn in Central Arkansas

Bare spots and thinning turf are one of the most common lawn complaints we hear around Hot Springs. The key is to figure out why a spot keeps failing before you throw grass at it — otherwise it comes right back.

First, find the real cause

  • Too much shade — warm-season grass thins out under heavy tree cover.
  • Compacted or rocky soil — roots can't get established, so grass stays weak.
  • Drainage — spots that stay soggy or wash out won't hold turf.
  • Heavy traffic — pet runs, paths, and play areas get worn bare.
  • Pests or disease — grubs and fungus can kill patches from below.

Fixing the cause is what makes the repair stick.

Small bare spots: repair them

For a few small patches, loosen the soil, level it, and lay a piece of fresh sod or seed the spot and keep it watered. Matching the repair to your existing grass type matters — patching zoysia with fescue just looks worse.

Widespread thinning: refresh or re-sod

If more than about a third of the lawn is thin or dead, spot repairs won't get you there. It's usually faster and better-looking to prep and lay fresh sod for an even, weed-free result you can enjoy right away rather than babying seed for a season.

When shade is the problem

Some spots are just too shady for turf, no matter what you do. In those cases the right fix is a design change — a shade-tolerant grass, a mulched bed, a stepping-stone path, or ground cover. We can help you decide whether to keep fighting for grass or turn the spot into something that actually thrives.
