Retaining walls in Central Arkansas: when you need one & what they cost

A lot of properties around Hot Springs sit on slopes and rocky, shifting soil — which is exactly where a retaining wall earns its keep. Here's when a wall is the right call, your options, and what affects the price.

Signs you need a retaining wall

  • A slope that's eroding, washing out, or losing soil after heavy rain.
  • Water running toward your house or pooling at the base of a hill.
  • A steep yard you'd like to level into usable, flat space (patio, garden, play area).
  • A bank that's hard to mow or keeps sliding.

Material options

Segmental block walls are engineered, consistent, and cost-effective. Natural stone gives a timeless, high-end look. And poured or carved concrete can be shaped and finished to look like stone while acting as one solid, strong structure — our specialty. The right choice depends on wall height, the load behind it, drainage, and the look you want.

What drives the cost

Retaining walls are priced by the project, not a flat rate, because the variables matter: height (taller walls carry more load and may need engineering), length, material, site access, and — most importantly — drainage. A wall that isn't drained properly behind the face will eventually fail, so good ones include gravel backfill and drainage built in. That's not where to cut corners.

Function and looks together

Because we design and build, we treat a retaining wall as part of the whole landscape — solving the drainage and erosion problem while making the wall a feature, not an eyesore. With carved concrete we can even match it to the character of the rest of your outdoor space. The best next step is to walk the slope with us and talk through options.
