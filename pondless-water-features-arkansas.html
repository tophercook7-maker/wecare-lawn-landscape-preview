Pondless water features in Arkansas: cost, upkeep & ideas

A water feature adds the one thing most backyards are missing — the sound of moving water — without the pond. If you love the idea of a waterfall or stream but not the maintenance of an open pond, a pondless water feature is usually the answer.

What "pondless" means

Instead of an open pool of standing water, the water recirculates: it flows over the falls or stream, then disappears into a gravel-filled reservoir hidden underground, where a pump sends it back to the top. You get the movement and sound of a waterfall with no open water to net, no standing water for mosquitoes, and no exposed pond edge.

Why it's lower maintenance

Because the water is hidden in the reservoir and constantly circulating, there's far less algae and debris to deal with than an open pond, and nothing for kids or pets to fall into. Upkeep is mostly topping off water lost to evaporation and an occasional cleanout. It's the low-fuss way to get a water feature.

What it costs

Pondless features are priced by the project — size and height of the falls, the stonework, how much rock and plumbing are involved, and access to the spot all factor in. A small, tucked-in feature is a modest project; a large multi-tier waterfall built into a slope is a bigger one. The best way to get a real number is to show us the space and the look you're after.

Where it fits

These look their best when they're integrated into the landscape rather than dropped in — spilling down a natural grade, tied into a patio or seating area, and lit for the evening.
