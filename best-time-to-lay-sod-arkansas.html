The best time to lay sod in Arkansas

The short answer: you can lay sod almost any time the ground isn't frozen — but the easiest window in Central Arkansas is mid-spring through early fall, when the soil is warm and the grass is actively growing. Here's how each season plays out around Hot Springs.

Spring (April–June): the sweet spot

Warm-season grasses like the Matrella zoysia we grow root fastest when soil temperatures are up and the plant is heading into its growing season. Late spring gives new sod months of warm weather to establish before winter, with generally reliable rainfall to help. If you can pick any time, this is it.

Summer (July–August): works with watering

Summer sodding is common here and works well — the catch is water. Fresh sod in July heat needs to be watered thoroughly right after it's laid and kept consistently moist for the first couple of weeks while it roots. If you can commit to that (or have us handle install and set your watering plan), summer is perfectly fine.

Early fall (September–October): a strong second window

Early fall still has warm soil but milder air, so new sod isn't fighting peak heat while it establishes. It's a great time to lay zoysia as long as it goes down early enough to root before the first hard frost.

Winter: possible, but wait if you can

Warm-season sod goes dormant (brown) in winter and won't actively root until the soil warms again. It can be laid on unfrozen ground and will green up in spring, but if your project isn't urgent, waiting for spring gives you a faster, more even result.

The bigger factor: prep and water

Honestly, how you lay sod matters more than the exact week. Good soil prep, tight seams, rolling it to press out air pockets, and watering right away are what make sod knit down and last — in any season.
